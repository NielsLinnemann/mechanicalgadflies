About the Workshop

Rationale

Placeholder text — to be revised

Can an AI system produce philosophy worth reading, and if it can, what should philosophers do about it?

This workshop grows out of AutoPhil, a collaboration on the automation of philosophical research. Over the past year, AI systems have moved from summarising philosophical literature to drafting full papers, reconstructing arguments, and running autonomous research loops with little human guidance. A philosophy article largely written by an AI agent has already appeared in a leading journal, and benchmark projects are now testing whether AI can generate genuinely novel philosophical ideas instead of merely restating existing ones.

These developments raise two families of questions. The first is practical: what does it take to build and use systems that do philosophy? Which pipelines, prompting strategies, agent architectures, argument-mapping tools, and evaluation methods actually work, and where do they fail? The second is foundational: what would it mean for philosophy, as a discipline and a practice, to be partly automated? Is philosophical understanding something that can be delegated? What happens to authorship, credit, and peer review? And what is lost, or gained, when the slow work of thinking is accelerated?

The workshop takes up both families in turn. Day one is devoted to the how-to: researchers who have built or used AI systems for philosophical work present their methods, results, and failures, followed by a hands-on session. Day two turns to the why, and whether: the epistemic, methodological, and normative questions raised by automating philosophy, including who counts as the author of AI-assisted work, and what philosophers should make of it.

Day 1

The How-To

Building, using and testing AI systems that do philosophy: agents that draft papers, map arguments and run research with varying degrees of autonomy, plus a hands-on lab.

Day 2

The Why (and Whether)

What automation means for philosophical understanding, authorship and the discipline, and whether, how far, and for whom we should want it.
